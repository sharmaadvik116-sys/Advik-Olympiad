What Is Earth Observation?

Earth observation uses remote sensing instruments to collect information about Earth's land, water, atmosphere, and changing environments.

Key idea: Remote sensing allows scientists to monitor large areas repeatedly without directly visiting every location.

Aerosols

Aerosols are small particles suspended in the atmosphere. They can absorb and scatter electromagnetic radiation.

These effects can influence what a remote sensing instrument measures.

Albedo

Albedo describes how much incoming radiation a surface reflects. Different surfaces have different reflective properties.

Surface Temperature

Thermal infrared observations can provide information related to surface temperature.

Different materials and environments can have different thermal behavior.

Vegetation Changes

Remote sensing can be used to observe changes in forests, agriculture, and other vegetation.

Comparing images collected at different times can reveal changes in vegetation patterns.

Geohazards

  • Volcanic activity
  • Landslides
  • Flooding
  • Wildfires

Remote sensing can help identify affected areas and monitor changes before, during, or after hazardous events.

Earth Processes

Remote sensing can help scientists study processes such as:

  • Glacial movement
  • River and stream patterns
  • Changes in land elevation
  • Surface deformation
