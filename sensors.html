What Is a Remote Sensing Sensor?

A remote sensing sensor detects electromagnetic radiation or other signals from a target and records information that can be used to study Earth's surface or atmosphere.

Sensors can be grouped into two major categories: passive sensors and active sensors.

Key idea:

Passive sensors detect radiation that already exists. Active sensors send out their own signal and measure the response from the target.

Passive Sensors

Passive sensors measure naturally occurring electromagnetic radiation. They do not need to transmit their own energy toward the target.

Important Passive Sensing Ranges

  • Ultraviolet (UV)
  • Visible light
  • Near-infrared (NIR)
  • Shortwave infrared (SWIR)
  • Thermal infrared (TIR)
Example:

An optical sensor can measure reflected sunlight from Earth's surface. The amount of reflected radiation can vary between vegetation, water, soil, and built surfaces.

Optical Sensors

Optical sensors detect electromagnetic radiation in wavelength ranges that can provide information about the appearance and properties of Earth's surface.

Multispectral optical systems can collect measurements in multiple wavelength bands. Comparing those bands can help distinguish different surface materials.

Remember:

Different materials interact with different wavelengths differently. That difference can be used to identify or classify features.

Thermal Infrared Sensors

Thermal infrared sensors measure radiation associated with the thermal state of surfaces.

Thermal measurements can be used to study differences in surface temperature and thermal behavior.

Example:

Two surfaces may have different temperatures even when they receive similar sunlight. A thermal sensor can detect differences in their emitted thermal radiation.

Active Sensors

Active sensors transmit their own signal toward a target and then measure the returned signal.

Because the sensor supplies the signal, active sensing can operate without depending entirely on naturally available illumination.

Important Active Sensor Types

  • Radar backscatter
  • Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
  • Interferometric SAR (InSAR)
  • Radar altimetry
  • Radar-derived elevation products

Radar

Radar uses electromagnetic waves to transmit energy toward a target and detect the returned signal.

The returned signal can provide information about the surface, including characteristics that affect the strength of the returned signal.

Radar Backscatter

Radar backscatter refers to the portion of the transmitted radar signal that is scattered back toward the sensor.

Think:

Stronger or weaker backscatter can provide information about the interaction between radar energy and the surface.

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)

SAR is a radar imaging technique that uses the motion of the sensor relative to the ground to produce detailed images.

SAR is an active sensing system and can collect data without depending on sunlight in the same way optical sensors do.

Science Olympiad focus:

Know that SAR is an active radar imaging system and understand the basic idea of radar backscatter and radar image interpretation.

Interferometric SAR (InSAR)

InSAR uses information from radar observations to analyze differences between radar measurements.

At the Science Olympiad level, focus on the basic interpretation of how InSAR can be used to detect changes in Earth's surface.

Example:

InSAR can be used to study surface deformation such as changes in elevation or subsidence.

Radar Altimetry

Radar altimetry uses radar signals to measure the distance between the sensor and a surface.

The measured range can be used to determine information about surface elevation or height relative to a reference.

Important:

Radar altimetry is specifically included in the Remote Sensing event scope, including the radar altimetry range relationship.

Radar-Derived Elevation Products

Radar observations can be processed to create information about Earth's elevation and surface shape.

These elevation products can be useful for mapping terrain and studying changes in the Earth's surface.

Remote Sensing Instruments

The Remote Sensing event includes several types of instruments and systems.

  • Scanning systems
  • Multispectral imaging systems
  • Optical and infrared imagers
  • Radiometers
  • Radar altimeters
  • Thermal infrared imagers
  • Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) instruments
  • Digital Elevation Models (DEMs)

Passive vs. Active Sensors

Feature Passive Sensor Active Sensor
Energy source Detects naturally available radiation. Transmits its own signal.
Examples Optical and thermal sensors. Radar, SAR, InSAR, and radar altimetry.
Important concept Measures radiation already present. Measures the response to a transmitted signal.

Science Olympiad Quick Review

  • Passive sensors detect naturally occurring radiation.
  • Active sensors transmit their own signal.
  • Optical sensors can measure reflected electromagnetic radiation.
  • Thermal infrared sensors measure thermal radiation.
  • Radar is an active sensing technique.
  • SAR is an active radar imaging system.
  • InSAR can be interpreted at a basic level for studying surface changes.
  • Radar altimetry measures range between the sensor and a surface.
  • Radar-derived elevation products provide information about surface elevation.
