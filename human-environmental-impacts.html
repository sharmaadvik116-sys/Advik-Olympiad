Remote Sensing and Human Activity

Remote sensing can be used to monitor how human activities change Earth's surface and surrounding environments.

Key idea: Comparing remote sensing data from different times can reveal changes in land cover and environmental conditions.

Habitat Loss

Changes in forests, wetlands, grasslands, and other ecosystems can reduce or fragment habitat.

Remote sensing can help identify where these changes are occurring.

Urbanization

Urbanization changes land from natural or agricultural surfaces to buildings, roads, parking areas, and other developed features.

Satellite imagery can be used to monitor the expansion of developed areas.

Deforestation

Deforestation involves the removal or substantial reduction of forest cover.

Remote sensing can help detect changes in vegetation over large areas and over long periods of time.

Land-Cover Change

Land-cover change occurs when one surface category changes into another.

Example: Forest → agricultural land
Agricultural land → developed land
Vegetation → burned area

Damage Assessment

Remote sensing can be used to compare conditions before and after events such as floods, wildfires, landslides, and other hazards.

This can help identify affected regions and monitor recovery.

Monitoring Change

A powerful remote sensing approach is to compare images collected at different times.

  • Before vs. after a wildfire
  • Before vs. after flooding
  • Forest change over time
  • Urban expansion
  • Changes in agricultural areas
