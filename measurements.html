Measuring Features in Remote Sensing Data

Remote sensing images and maps can be used to estimate distances, areas, elevations, slopes, and other geographic properties.

Key idea: Always pay attention to the map scale and units before calculating.

Distance

Distance measures the separation between two features. A map scale can be used to convert a measured map distance into an estimated real-world distance.

Perimeter

Perimeter is the total distance around a feature or region.

For an irregular feature, the boundary may be measured in several small segments and added together.

Area

Area describes the amount of surface covered by a feature.

Remote sensing can be used to estimate the area of forests, lakes, burned regions, urban areas, agricultural fields, and other land-cover features.

Altitude and Elevation

Elevation describes the height of a location relative to a reference surface. Remote sensing products can provide elevation information.

Digital Elevation Models, or DEMs, are commonly used to represent elevation across an area.

Slope and Gradient

Slope describes how quickly elevation changes over horizontal distance.

Example: A steep mountain side has a larger elevation change over a given horizontal distance than a nearly flat area.
