Land Cover Classification

Science Olympiad Division B — Remote Sensing

Season 2026–2027

What Is Land Cover Classification?

Land cover classification organizes areas of Earth's surface into categories based on their observed characteristics.

Examples include forests, water, agriculture, developed areas, and other surface types.

Why Use Remote Sensing?

Different materials interact with electromagnetic radiation in different ways. Remote sensing measurements can therefore provide information that helps distinguish land-cover types.

Key idea: Classification converts remote sensing observations into meaningful categories.

Supervised Classification

In supervised classification, the classification process uses examples of areas whose classes are already known.

These known examples help the system identify similar observations elsewhere in the image.

Unsupervised Classification

In unsupervised classification, the system groups observations according to similarities in the data without requiring every group to be defined in advance by the user.

The resulting groups can then be interpreted and assigned meaningful land-cover categories.

Comparing the Two

Method Main idea
Supervised Uses known training examples
Unsupervised Groups observations based on similarities in the data

Land Cover Change

Comparing classification results from different dates can reveal changes in land cover.

For example, an area may change from vegetation to developed land.
